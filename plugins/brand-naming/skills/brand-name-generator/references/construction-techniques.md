# Construction Techniques

Every name is built with one of these methods. In each generation wave, spread
candidates across **at least 6** of them — variety in construction is what
prevents a wave from collapsing into obvious portmanteaus of the brief's keywords.

| # | Technique | How it's built | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real word** | Existing dictionary word, used as-is | Dove, Apple, Oracle, Virgin |
| 2 | **Compound** | Two real words joined | Salesforce, Facebook, YouTube, FedEx, Snapchat |
| 3 | **Portmanteau / blend** | Parts of two words merged | Microsoft, Spotify, Pinterest, Verizon (veritas+horizon), Vodafone |
| 4 | **Altered spelling** | Real word, modified letters for distinctiveness/domains | Flickr, Tumblr, Fiverr, Lyft, Froot Loops |
| 5 | **Invented / coined** | Fabricated from phonemes, no prior meaning | Kodak, Xerox, Verizon, Zappos (zapatos+zap) |
| 6 | **Foreign-word mining** | A word from another language carrying the meaning | Vox (L. voice), Nexus (L. connection), Audi (L. "listen"), Clio (Gk muse) |
| 7 | **Phonetic construction** | Built from desirable sound patterns (strong onset, clean stop) | Stripe, Zara, Kleenex |
| 8 | **Metaphor / narrative** | An apt object/creature/place as an unexpected metaphor | Amazon (scale), Oracle (foresight), Slack (no tension in the line), Nike |
| 9 | **Eponymous / mythological / geographic** | Person, myth, or place | Tesla, Adidas (Adi Dassler), Hermes, Patagonia, Nokia |
| 10 | **Two-word cross ("idea × idea")** | A base word crossed with a second word that adds an *angle* — tension, context, or unexpected juxtaposition. Built in two stages (see below). | The North Face, Red Bull, Free People, Teenage Engineering, Patagonia Provisions, Crowd Strike, Notion Calendar |

> Techniques 1–9 build a **single token**. Technique 10 builds a **multi-word
> name** — and most successful modern brands are *not* one word. Always generate
> both: a wave that is 100% one-word coinages is a failure of coverage, not a
> stylistic choice. See "Word-count diversity" below and `name-memorability.md`.

## Generation tactics per technique

- **Real word / arbitrary:** scan the conceptual territory for evocative real
  words with *no* logical tie to the category. The disconnect is the strength.
- **Portmanteau:** take one word from territory A and one from territory B; try
  head+tail, tail+head, and overlapping-syllable merges. Reject Frankenword seams
  that are hard to say.
- **Altered spelling:** drop a vowel, swap `c→k`/`s→z`, double a terminal
  consonant, add `-r`/`-ly`/`-ify`. Watch that the result is still spellable from
  hearing it (SCRATCH "spelling challenge").
- **Coined:** assemble CV/CVC syllables with a strong initial consonant and a
  clean terminal stop; lean on sound-symbolic affixes (`-ix`, `-ex`, `-on`, `-a`,
  `-o`). Pronounce each aloud mentally before keeping it.
- **Foreign-word mining:** Latin, Greek, Sanskrit, and Nordic roots are productive
  and feel premium. Verify the word isn't vulgar/loaded in its source language.
- **Metaphor:** explore the *experience* of the product (warmth, arrival, trust,
  discovery), then find the object/creature that embodies it.

## The two-stage cross ("idea × idea") — technique 10 in detail

The most reliable way to produce a *memorable two-word name* is a deliberate
two-stage move, not a single brainstorm:

1. **Stage 1 — the first name (the anchor).** Pick a strong base token: a real
   word, a coinage, a metaphor, or one of your best single-word survivors from an
   earlier wave. This is the noun the brand stands on. Examples of anchors: *Face*,
   *Bull*, *People*, *Engineering*, *Strike*.
2. **Stage 2 — cross it with an angle.** Add a second word that bends the anchor's
   meaning — a modifier that supplies tension, a domain, a posture, or an
   unexpected pairing. The two words should "bounce off one another": *North* ×
   *Face*, *Red* × *Bull*, *Free* × *People*, *Teenage* × *Engineering*. The result
   says **idea × idea** (multiplicative, a small story) rather than **idea + idea**
   (additive, a description). Neither word should be able to carry the brand alone.

Where to source the **angle** in Stage 2:
- The conceptual territories you mapped (cross territory A's anchor with territory
  B's modifier — the cross-territory blend is where surprise lives).
- The brand's *posture* or *promise* (free, bold, quiet, north, true, open).
- **The proven patterns of comparable successful startups/brands** — study how
  names in or near the category pair words (place + object, attitude + craft,
  scale + thing), then build a *fresh* cross in the same register. This is borrowing
  the **pattern**, never the words — the result must pass target verification
  (distinct from, not derivative of, any existing startup; see
  `scoring-rubric.md` §Target verification and `process-stages.md` Stage 3).

Keep the cross sayable: two short words beat one long word + one long word. Watch
the run-together reading (a two-word name often ships as one lowercased domain/slug
— `thenorthface`, `redbull` — so check that the seam doesn't create a misread).

## Word-count diversity (verify every wave)

A wave must not collapse into only one-word names. Spread each wave roughly:

- **~50–60% one-word** (techniques 1–9): real words, coinages, blends.
- **~35–45% two-word** (technique 10): the idea × idea cross.
- **~5–10% three-word** only where the rhythm clearly earns it (*Teenage
  Engineering* is two; *Palo Alto Networks* is three — three is the ceiling).

Before scoring a wave, **verify the spread** — if it is essentially all one-word
candidates, the wave is incomplete: generate a two-word cross pass before
filtering. Two-word names also clear trademark and domain screening far more often
than single coined tokens, so under-generating them quietly starves the funnel.

## Anti-pattern

Do not derive the whole wave from the brief's literal nouns. "Fast food delivery"
→ explore *warmth, arrival, anticipation, satisfaction*, not "fast/food/deliver".
Large keyword-anchored brainstorms converge on comfortable, generic names; small
constrained explorations across territories produce distinctive ones.
